1. Definition and Origins of Cyber Crime 
Definition 
 
Cyber crime refers to any illegal activity in which a computer, computer network, digital device, 
or the Internet is used as a tool, target, or medium for committing a crime. 
 

=== PAGE 2 ===
Simple definition 
 
Cyber crime = Crime committed using or against computers, networks, or digital systems. 
 
Examples include: 
 
Hacking 
Phishing 
Identity theft 
Online fraud 
Malware attacks 
Cyber stalking 
Ransomware 
Data theft 
Denial-of-Service attacks 
Important point 
 
A computer can play three different roles in cyber crime: 
 
Role Meaning Example 
Target Computer/system itself is attacked Hacking a server 
Tool Computer is used to commit crime Online banking fraud 
Storage Computer stores illegal/stolen information Storing stolen databases 
Origins of Cyber Crime 
 
Cyber crime developed along with the development of computers and the Internet. 

=== PAGE 3 ===
 
1. Early computer misuse 
 
During the 1960s and 1970s, computers were mainly used by governments, universities and 
large organizations. 
 
Unauthorized access to computer systems began appearing as computer networks became 
more common. 
 
2. Phone/Phreaking era 
 
Before widespread Internet usage, criminals experimented with telephone systems. 
 
This activity became known as phreaking. 
 
Attackers attempted to manipulate telephone networks to obtain unauthorized access or free 
services. 
 
3. Computer viruses 
 
During the 1980s, computer viruses became an important form of computer-related crime. 
 
A virus could: 
 
Replicate itself 
Infect other files 
Damage systems 

=== PAGE 4 ===
Modify data 
Disrupt computer operations 
4. Internet expansion 
 
During the 1990s, Internet usage expanded rapidly. 
 
This created new opportunities for: 
 
Email fraud 
Hacking 
Website attacks 
Online scams 
Identity theft 
5. Modern cyber crime 
 
With smartphones, cloud computing, social media, cryptocurrency and IoT , cyber crime has 
become more sophisticated. 
 
Modern attacks include: 
 
Ransomware 
Advanced phishing 
Cryptocurrency scams 
Supply-chain attacks 
Social engineering 
State-sponsored cyber attacks 

=== PAGE 5 ===
Deepfake-enabled fraud 
Characteristics of Cyber Crime 
Technology dependent 
Can be committed from anywhere 
Criminals can hide their identity 
Can affect millions of people simultaneously 
Evidence is mainly digital 
Attackers can operate across international borders 
Crimes can happen very quickly 
Investigation requires specialized technical knowledge 
Conclusion 
 
Cyber crime originated with the misuse of early computer and communication systems and has 
evolved with technological development. Today, it is a global problem affecting individuals, 
businesses, governments and society. 
 
Exam ending line 
 
Thus, the growth of digital technology has created enormous benefits but has also created new 
opportunities for criminals, making cyber crime one of the major challenges of the digital age. 
 
2. Introduction to Cyber Crime 
Meaning 
 
Cyber crime is a category of crime involving computers, networks, digital devices and the 
Internet. 
 

=== PAGE 6 ===
Unlike traditional crimes, cyber crimes can often be conducted without the criminal being 
physically present at the location of the victim. 
 
Example 
 
A criminal sitting in one country can steal money from the bank account of a person in another 
country using the Internet. 
 
Why Cyber Crime is Increasing 
1. Rapid Internet growth 
 
Millions of people use the Internet for: 
 
Banking 
Shopping 
Education 
Communication 
Entertainment 
 
This provides criminals with a large target population. 
 
2. Digitalization 
 
Organizations increasingly store information digitally. 
 
Examples: 
 

=== PAGE 7 ===
Customer information 
Bank information 
Medical records 
Government records 
Business secrets 
 
Therefore, valuable information has become a target. 
 
3. Easy availability of technology 
 
Attackers can obtain sophisticated tools and information online. 
 
4. Anonymity 
 
Criminals can use techniques such as: 
 
Fake accounts 
Compromised systems 
Proxy servers 
Other anonymizing technologies 
 
to make investigation more difficult. 
 
5. Financial motivation 
 
A large portion of cyber crime is financially motivated. 

=== PAGE 8 ===
 
Examples: 
 
Banking fraud 
Ransomware 
Credit-card fraud 
Cryptocurrency scams 
Common Types 
Hacking 
 
Unauthorized access to a computer or network. 
 
Phishing 
 
Fake emails/messages/websites are used to trick victims into revealing information. 
 
Malware 
 
Malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, spy on, or gain unauthorized access to 
systems. 
 
Ransomware 
 
Malware that makes data or systems inaccessible and demands payment. 
 
Identity theft

Using another person's personal information without authorization. 
 
Cyber stalking 
 
Repeated unwanted monitoring or harassment through digital technologies.

4. Global Perspective on Cyber Crime 
Introduction 
 
Cyber crime is no longer limited to one country or region. 
 
The Internet connects computers across the world, allowing criminals to attack victims 
regardless of geographical boundaries. 
 
Therefore: 
 
Cyber crime is a global problem requiring global cooperation. 
 
Why Cyber Crime is Global 
1. Global Internet Connectivity 
 
An attacker can operate remotely from another country. 
 
2. International Criminal Networks 
 
Cyber criminals can work together across different countries. 
 
3. International Victims 
 
One attack can affect users from multiple countries. 
 

=== PAGE 19 ===
4. Cross-border infrastructure 
 
Attackers may use systems located in several countries during a single operation. 
 
For example: 
 
Attacker 
   ↓ 
Compromised System A 
   ↓ 
Compromised System B 
   ↓ 
Victim Server 
 
The attacker and victim may be in completely different countries. 
 
Global Impact 
Economic impact 
 
Cyber crime causes: 
 
Financial fraud 
Business interruption 
Data recovery costs 
Cybersecurity expenditure 
National security 

=== PAGE 20 ===
 
Governments must protect: 
 
Military systems 
Government databases 
Critical infrastructure 
Privacy 
 
Personal information can be stolen and misused internationally. 
 
Challenges 
Different laws in different countries 
Difficulty identifying attackers 
Jurisdiction problems 
Different investigation procedures 
Difficulty collecting evidence from another country 
Rapid technological development 
5. Cyber Crime Transcends National Boundaries 
 
This is an important 10–15 mark question. 
 
Meaning 
 
A cyber crime is said to transcend national boundaries when different parts of the crime occur 
in different countries. 
 

=== PAGE 21 ===
Unlike traditional crimes, physical distance is often not a major barrier. 
 
Example 
 
Suppose: 
 
Criminal is in Country A 
Server is in Country B 
Victim is in Country C 
Stolen money goes to Country D 
 
Then: 
 
Country A 
Attacker 
   ↓ 
Country B 
Server 
   ↓ 
Country C 
Victim 
   ↓ 
Country D 
Financial destination 
 
One cyber crime can therefore involve four jurisdictions. 

=== PAGE 22 ===
 
Why This Creates Problems 
1. Jurisdiction 
 
Which country's police should investigate? 
 
2. Different laws 
 
An action may be illegal in one country but treated differently in another. 
 
3. Evidence collection 
 
Digital evidence may be stored on servers in another country. 
 
4. Attribution 
 
Identifying the actual attacker can be difficult. 
 
5. International cooperation 
 
Authorities may need assistance from foreign agencies. 
 
Solution 
 
Countries need: 
 

=== PAGE 23 ===
International treaties 
Information sharing 
Mutual legal assistance 
Joint investigations 
Cybersecurity cooperation 
Common legal frameworks 
Digital forensic cooperation 
Conclusion 
 
The borderless nature of cyberspace makes cyber crime a transnational problem, and effective 
investigation requires cooperation between countries.

8. Case Study – Global Cybersecurity Struggle 
SolarWinds Supply-Chain Incident 
 
The SolarWinds incident is an important example of the difficulty of defending interconnected 
digital systems. 
 
Attackers compromised the software supply chain associated with SolarWinds' Orion platform 
and inserted malicious code into legitimate software updates. 
 
Organizations that installed affected updates could consequently become compromised. 
 
Why it was significant 
 
It demonstrated that: 
 
Organizations can be attacked through trusted suppliers. 
Software supply chains are important security risks. 
Cybersecurity cannot focus only on an organization's internal network. 
International cooperation and information sharing are important. 
Lesson 
 
Modern cybersecurity requires protection of the entire ecosystem: 
 

=== PAGE 28 ===
Organization 
     ↓ 
Software 
     ↓ 
Supplier 
     ↓ 
Cloud services 
     ↓ 
Third-party providers 
     ↓ 
Users 
 
Security at only one point is not sufficient. 
 
9. International Cooperation to Combat Cyber Crime 
Introduction 
 
Because cyber crime can cross national boundaries, a single country cannot always investigate 
and prosecute a cyber criminal alone. 
 
Therefore, international cooperation is essential. 
 
Major Forms of Cooperation 
1. Information Sharing 
 
Countries and cybersecurity organizations exchange information about: 

=== PAGE 29 ===
 
Threats 
Malware 
Attack techniques 
Indicators of compromise 
2. Mutual Legal Assistance 
 
A country may request legal assistance from another country to obtain: 
 
Digital evidence 
Records 
Witness information 
Other investigative assistance 
3. Joint Investigations 
 
Law enforcement agencies from different countries may cooperate on international cybercrime 
investigations. 
 
4. Extradition 
 
If legally permitted, a suspected criminal may be transferred to another country for prosecution. 
 
5. International Treaties 
 
International legal frameworks help countries cooperate in cybercrime investigations.

Benefits

11. Planning of Cyber Offences by Cyber Criminals 
 
This is another very important 10–15 mark question. 
 
Definition 
 
Planning of a cyber offence refers to the process through which criminals prepare, organize and 
execute a cyber attack. 
 
Cyber criminals generally do not randomly attack every target. Many attacks involve some level 
of preparation. 
 
Stages of Planning a Cyber Offence 
Stage 1 – Target Selection 
 
The criminal first identifies a target. 
 
Possible targets: 
 

=== PAGE 33 ===
Individual 
Company 
Government organization 
Bank 
Website 
Network 
Factors criminals may consider 
Value of information 
Security weaknesses 
Financial potential 
Accessibility 
Stage 2 – Information Gathering 
 
The attacker collects information about the target. 
 
This is often called reconnaissance. 
 
Information may include: 
 
Publicly available information 
Organization structure 
Technology used 
Public contact information 
Domain information 
Example 
 

=== PAGE 34 ===
An attacker may examine publicly available information about an organization's employees and 
technology. 
 
Stage 3 – Identifying Vulnerabilities 
 
The attacker looks for weaknesses in: 
 
Software 
Systems 
Network configurations 
Human behavior 
Authentication practices 
 
The objective is to identify a possible entry point. 
 
Stage 4 – Choosing Attack Method 
 
Depending on the target, criminals may choose methods such as: 
 
Phishing 
Malware 
Credential theft 
Social engineering 
Exploitation of vulnerabilities 
Account takeover 
Stage 5 – Preparation 

=== PAGE 35 ===
 
The attacker prepares the required resources. 
 
These may include: 
 
Malicious software 
Fake communication 
Compromised accounts 
Infrastructure used to conduct the attack 
Stage 6 – Initial Access 
 
The attacker attempts to gain unauthorized access. 
 
Common examples: 
 
Phishing 
   ↓ 
Victim provides credentials 
   ↓ 
Attacker obtains account access 
 
or 
 
Unpatched system 
       ↓ 
Vulnerability exploited 

=== PAGE 36 ===
       ↓ 
Unauthorized access 
Stage 7 – Establishing Persistence 
 
After obtaining access, an attacker may attempt to maintain unauthorized access. 
 
The goal is to prevent losing access if the initial entry point is closed. 
 
Stage 8 – Privilege Escalation 
 
The attacker may attempt to obtain higher privileges. 
 
For example: 
 
Normal account 
      ↓ 
Higher privileges 
      ↓ 
Administrative access 
 
Higher privileges can provide greater control over systems. 
 
Stage 9 – Achieving the Objective 
 
The attacker's objective depends on the type of crime. 
 

=== PAGE 37 ===
Examples: 
 
Financial crime 
 
Steal money. 
 
Data theft 
 
Steal confidential information. 
 
Ransomware 
 
Disrupt access to data and demand payment. 
 
Espionage 
 
Obtain sensitive information. 
 
Sabotage 
 
Disrupt systems or services. 
 
Stage 10 – Concealing Activity 
 
Criminals may attempt to make investigation difficult by: 
 

=== PAGE 38 ===
Using compromised infrastructure 
Deleting or manipulating evidence 
Using multiple systems 
Hiding malicious activity among legitimate traffic 
 
For exam purposes, remember this as the concealment stage.

  How to Write a 10–15 Mark Answer 
 
For almost every topic in this unit, use this structure: 
 
1. Definition – 2 marks 
 
Start with a clear definition. 
 
2. Introduction – 1–2 marks 
 
Explain why the topic is important. 
 
3. Main explanation – 5–7 marks 

=== PAGE 42 ===
 
Use headings + numbered points. 
 
4. Example/Case Study – 2–3 marks 
 
Give a relevant real-world example such as WannaCry, SolarWinds or Emotet where 
appropriate. 
 
5. Diagram/Flowchart – 1–2 marks 
 
Draw a simple flowchart. 
 
6. Conclusion – 1 mark 
 
End with 2–3 strong lines. 
 
Golden formula: 
 
Definition → Introduction → Types/Stages → Explanation → Example → Effects → Diagram → 
Conclusion 
 
This format is particularly useful for your GTU-style 10–15 mark questions because you can 
expand or shorten each section according to the marks. 
 
If you are preparing this specifically for your mid-sem, the highest-value topics to learn
