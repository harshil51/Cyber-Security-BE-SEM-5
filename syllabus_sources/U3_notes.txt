Complete 10–15 Marks Exam Notes

1. Cyber Crime with Mobile and Wireless Devices 
Introduction 
A mobile device is a portable electronic device such as a smartphone, tablet, smartwatch, etc. 
that can connect to the Internet or wireless networks. 
Cyber crime with mobile and wireless devices refers to illegal activities performed using mobile 
phones, wireless networks or targeting mobile devices to steal information, money, identity or 
services. 
Examples: 
• Mobile banking fraud 
• UPI/payment fraud 
• SIM swapping 
• Phishing through SMS 
• Malicious mobile applications 
• Identity theft 
• Wi-Fi attacks 
• Bluetooth attacks 
• Data theft 
• Mobile ransomware

Common Mobile Cyber Crimes 
1. Mobile Malware 
Malicious software installed on a mobile device to steal information or damage the device. 
Examples: 
• Spyware 
• Trojan 
• Ransomware 
• Banking malware 
2. Phishing and Smishing 
Phishing uses fake websites/emails. 
Smishing uses fraudulent SMS messages. 
Example: 
"Your bank account will be blocked. Click here to verify." 
The link may lead to a fake banking website. 
3. SIM Swap Fraud 
An attacker obtains control of the victim's mobile number by transferring the number to 
another SIM. 

=== PAGE 3 ===
The attacker may then receive: 
• OTPs 
• Banking messages 
• Password-reset codes 
4. Mobile Payment Fraud 
Attackers may target: 
• UPI 
• Mobile banking 
• Digital wallets 
• Credit/debit cards 
5. Identity Theft 
Personal information such as: 
• Name 
• Mobile number 
• Aadhaar-related information 
• Email 
• Passwords 
may be stolen and misused. 
6. Bluetooth Attacks 
Attackers can attempt to exploit insecure Bluetooth connections to access or transfer 
information. 
7. Wi-Fi Attacks 
Attackers may create fake Wi-Fi networks or intercept poorly protected communications. 
 
Impact 
Mobile cyber crimes can result in: 

=== PAGE 4 ===
1. Financial loss 
2. Identity theft 
3. Privacy violation 
4. Loss of confidential data 
5. Reputation damage 
6. Unauthorized transactions 
7. Business losses 
8. Emotional stress 
9. Legal consequences 
 
Prevention 
• Use strong passwords/PINs. 
• Enable biometric authentication. 
• Install applications only from trusted sources. 
• Keep OS and applications updated. 
• Avoid unknown Wi-Fi networks. 
• Do not click suspicious SMS links. 
• Enable device encryption. 
• Use multi-factor authentication. 
• Lock the device automatically. 
• Enable remote tracking/wiping. 
• Review application permissions. 
Conclusion 
Mobile devices have become an important part of communication, banking and business. 
Therefore, securing mobile devices and wireless communication is essential to prevent financial, 
privacy and identity-related cyber crimes.

4. Credit Card Fraud – Types 
Definition 
Credit card fraud is the unauthorized use of a credit card or credit-card information to obtain 
money, goods or services. 
The attacker may steal: 
• Card number 
• CVV 
• Expiry date 
• PIN 
• OTP 
• Account credentials 
 
Types of Credit Card Fraud 
1. Lost or Stolen Card Fraud 
The physical card is lost or stolen and used by another person. 
2. Card-Not-Present Fraud 
The physical card is not required. 
It commonly occurs during: 
• Online shopping 
• Online subscriptions 
• Telephone transactions 
The attacker only needs stolen card information. 
3. Phishing 
Victim receives a fake email/SMS/website requesting card details. 
Example: 
Fake Bank Message 

=== PAGE 12 ===
       ↓ 
Fake Website 
       ↓ 
Card Details 
       ↓ 
Attacker 
4. Skimming 
A skimming device captures card information during a transaction. 
It can target: 
• ATMs 
• Payment terminals 
5. Identity Theft 
An attacker obtains personal information and uses it to create or misuse financial accounts. 
6. Account Takeover 
The attacker gains access to the victim's online banking/card account. 
7. Application Fraud 
Fraudulent personal information may be used to apply for a new credit card. 
8. Card Cloning 
Stolen card information can potentially be used to create a fraudulent duplicate card. 
 
5. Impact of Credit Card Fraud 
Credit card fraud affects individuals, banks and businesses. 
1. Financial Loss 
Unauthorized transactions can cause direct monetary loss. 
2. Identity Theft 
Personal and financial information may be misused. 

=== PAGE 13 ===
3. Credit Score Problems 
Fraudulent transactions or accounts may negatively affect the victim's credit history. 
4. Privacy Loss 
Sensitive financial information may be exposed. 
5. Business Loss 
Merchants and financial institutions may face fraud-related costs. 
6. Reputation Damage 
Banks and businesses can lose customer trust. 
7. Investigation Costs 
Organizations may spend significant resources investigating fraudulent activities.

1. Multi-Factor Authentication 
Use more than one authentication factor.

3. Secure Online Shopping

4. Never Share OTP 
Banks generally advise customers not to share OTPs, PINs or CVVs with others. 
5. Use Transaction Limits

Banks use machine-learning and rule-based systems to detect unusual transactions.

7. Card Blocking 
Immediately block the card if it is lost or stolen. 
8. Virtual/Tokenized Cards 
Tokenization replaces sensitive card information with a token. 
9. Regular Monitoring 
Check:

Credit card fraud can cause financial and privacy losses. Strong authentication, transaction 
monitoring, fraud detection and user awareness can significantly reduce the risk.

For almost every topic in this unit, use this structure: 
1. Definition – 1–2 marks 
Write a clear definition. 
2. Introduction – 1 mark 
Explain why the topic is important. 
3. Diagram – 1–2 marks 
Draw a simple flowchart/architecture. 
4. Main explanation – 5–6 marks 
Write 6–8 subheadings with 2–4 lines each.

6. Prevention/Security Measures – 2–3 marks

7. Conclusion – 1 mark 
End with 2–3 lines summarizing the topic.
